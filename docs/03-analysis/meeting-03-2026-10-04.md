## 2. Two defects he found by looking

### 2.1 The level count — `G+4` reported as 13 levels

> *"البودیوم 13 … هل ده 13؟ ده G plus 4 — أربعة أدوار. فهو المفروض إنه يطلع أربعة بس، مش 13."*
> — 41:11–41:31

He read `G+4` off the affection plan and the screen reported 13. This is the most serious
item in the meeting: it is a **capacity figure**, on the screen the product is sold on, and
it is wrong by a factor of three.

Two candidate causes, and they must be told apart before either is touched:

1. The FAR-driven floor count in the setback↔floor fixpoint is **not being capped by the
   stated `G+N`**, so the solver returns the floors FAR would buy rather than the floors the
   plan permits. If so, a plan that states `G+4` and a FAR that implies 13 is a
   **`CONDITIONAL`** situation at best and a refusal at worst — it is exactly the
   `DJAZ1MED12RES011` shape (a plan that states one limit and not another), and the governing
   band must be the stated height, with the FAR-implied count reported beside it, not
   instead of it.
2. The number on screen is a **different quantity than its label claims** — total levels
   including basements and podium parking, printed under a heading that reads as storeys.
   `LevelSchedule` exists precisely so these stop being confused, and `G+2P+8` being three
   podium levels rather than two was the same class of bug.

Either way the fix is in the engine and the label, never in the renderer.

### 2.2 The ramp climbs where it must descend

> *"العربية لو دخلت من هنا هتمشي وبعد كده الرامب هنا — شايفه؟ طلع لفوق. فهي غلط."* — 40:11–40:20

In the 3D view the ramp rises from the entry instead of falling to the parking level below.
`placeCars` and the ramp geometry are one code path shared by the sheet, the DXF and the
3D view, so a sign error in the ramp's level delta is wrong **in the DXF an architect
x-refs into a submission set**, not only on screen. Check the ramp's start and end level
against `LevelSchedule` — a ramp serving `B1` descends, one serving `P1` climbs, and a
basement default of zero (§3.1) means most schemes should descend.

**His memory was exactly right.** He said 33 m² for one and 55 m² for two at 59:29, from

room needing ventilation on two sides cannot sit in the middle of a floor plate.

**And the document contains a transcription trap that is not hypothetical.** The tables are
Illustrator artwork whose content stream does not run in reading order: extracted as text,
the single-room table's descriptions arrive as [4-and-above, 2×, extra, 1×] and its areas as

42** — a transformer room half its required size, on a figure that appears twice elsewhere in
the same table and therefore looks consistent. Every pairing was resolved by y-coordinate,
and `packages/rules/test/dewa-2017.test.ts` asserts against each mis-pairing *by name*.

Six clauses in Section 11 are **not** encoded and are listed in `DEWA_NOT_MECHANIZED` with

3.0 m × 3.0 m clear transformer-transport ramp at a maximum 1:10, which is a *second* ramp

layout.

> الرقم."* — 20:49–21:01
> *"لما تغيّر الأبعاد، يتغيّر الرسم هنا."* — 20:33

The feature is well specified and it is a **plot-intake editor**:

1. A map or an uploaded affection-plan image as an underlay, with an opacity control.
2. **Two-point scale calibration** — click two points, type the real distance (`30.51`), and
   the underlay scales to it.
3. Trace the boundary vertex by vertex on the underlay.
4. Click any **edge** and type its length; the ring updates.
5. The typed figure is authoritative — *"لو هي مش مظبوطة، أنا أظبّط الرقم"* (12:40–12:44).

The last point is the one that makes this consistent with everything else here, and it must
be built in from the start: a traced length is a **measurement off an image** and a typed
length is a **statement by a named user**. They are different provenance classes. A traced
edge is `ASSUMED` with the basis naming the underlay and its calibration; the moment he types
over it, that edge becomes `USER_SET` by him. A boundary that silently mixed the two would be
publishing an image-tracing as a surveyed dimension.

He also asked about Google Maps/Earth costs (~$200) and whether the plot area can come from
the map automatically (12:27–12:46). **It cannot, and it must not**: a polygon traced off
satellite imagery is not a cadastral boundary, and the whole product is built on refusing to
borrow a number. The underlay is a drafting aid; the figures come from the plan or from him.
