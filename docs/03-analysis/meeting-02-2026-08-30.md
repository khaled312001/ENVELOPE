And what he wants from the software: *"أنا عايز الـ software يقول لي حط المدخل فين… أدوس هنا
يقول لي إن المدخل من هنا أحسن"* (29:02).

The first reading of B.7.2.1 was that the ≥15 m junction clearance needs a road-network layer
nobody has. **That was wrong.** The clause measures it "from the chamfered edge of the plot" —
a vertex the engine already holds — and its other two constraints are plain edge attributes:

- Access is taken from a `ROAD` edge. A shared boundary is not an option, which is his own rule.
- *"If the building is facing more than one road, the vehicle access point should be from the
  **secondary** road, or as specified on the affection plan"* — and `RoadHierarchy` ranks
  exactly that, while the Trakhees sheets carry an "Access Side" box for the override.

So placement is derivable today: [`packages/capacity/src/access.ts`](../../packages/capacity/src/access.ts).
What genuinely is not derivable is whether a T junction sits *opposite* the chosen opening —
that needs the road network, and it is reported as NOT ASSESSED rather than blocking the rest.

## 9b. What the developer standards turned out to contain

Reading the three Azizi PDFs properly — `250617_UNIT AREAS, AMENITIES,` and the two
`Annexure A` project briefs — did more than supply data. It found a defect in the engine
and it answered a question the affection plan could not.

**It found a defect.** One line in §4.1 of the brief:

> *"GFA achievement is critical. Expectations are between 93%-97% (SA / GFA)."*

The engine computed units as `GFA ÷ saleable-area-per-unit`, which holds only if every
square metre of GFA sells. Cores, corridors, structure, plant and amenity are all inside
GFA and none of them does. The count came out 3–7% high on every run and nothing caught
it, because the 1.00 was implicit — there was no number anywhere to argue with. It is now
a required input with no default, and the screen refuses to compute until it is answered.

**It unblocked a plot.** `DJAZ1MED12RES011` prints `G+11` and no FAR; `blockingGaps()`
correctly stops the run. Its brief prints **FAR 5** and **Total GFA 11,829.35 m²**, and
5 × 2,365.87 reconciles exactly. So the brief is the second instrument that answers for a
plot the first one could not — a *different* instrument, issued by a developer's consultant
rather than by Trakhees, and marked as such wherever its values appear.

**It gave the parking work its benchmark.** *"Car park minimum efficiency should achieve:
37.5 m2 per car with no shortage"*, and *"Parking count to be 5% more than the minimum
required."* The layout already reports achieved area-per-bay; now it has the number the
client measures it against.

**And it named the thing the client said the skill was.** The mix tables are exactly what
turns a permitted GFA into a unit count:

| | Best case | Worst case |
|---|---|---|
| General standard | Studio 80-85%, 1-bed 18%, 2-bed 2% | Studio 70%, 1-bed 25%, 2-bed 3%, 3-bed 2% |
| Plot 5180178 / 5180196 | Studio 100% | Studio 90%, 1-bed 10% |

Two cautions are recorded in the code and worth repeating here:

- The tables are laid out **right-to-left in the content stream**. A naive text extraction
  reads the podium column as the tower one and swaps a 340 ft² studio cap for a 500 ft²
  one — a 47% error in the direction that inflates every studio-heavy scheme.
- On plot 5180196 the document's **own numbers do not reconcile**: 5.05 × 1,740.56 =
  8,789.83 against a printed 8,796.46. Neither is wrong — the true ratio is 5.0538, printed
  rounded. The GFA is what the developer priced; re-multiplying the rounded FAR
  under-reports by 6.63 m².

4. **Which developer standards.** Azizi is now **encoded** — unit areas, both mix scenarios,

   measure, and `notMechanized` says so on screen.
6. **RFP parsing.** The two RFP samples are read by hand, not by the engine. Only affection
   plans are parsed automatically.
7. **Intellectual property.** Khaled raised it at 25:02; the client waved it off —
